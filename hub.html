Overview

The project at a glance. Who, what, and what "done" looks like.

Goal

Coordinate the repair of Ben's Mitsubishi Pajero. Get it back on the road, legal and roadworthy, for a low cost.

The car

  • 2009 Mitsubishi Pajero VR-X
  • Diesel · NT Series · Australia

Who does the work

  • Ben does the work himself.
  • He has limited experience repairing cars.
  • He is keen. He wants to learn and enjoy this.

How we work

  • Plain, practical terms. Short sentences. The Hemingway way.
  • Break the work into small steps.
  • One task at a time. No pressure.

Damage

The tow bar pushed into the front of the Pajero. It crushed the grille, went through the radiator fans, and into the radiator.

  • Front grille crushed.
  • Radiator fans broken.
  • Radiator holed. A large amount of coolant spilled on the road. It needs replacing.
  • Bumper bar crushed.
  • Radiator support bracket badly bent — needs replacing. The radiator hangs off it.
  • Bonnet latch bent — now removed to open the bonnet.

Mechanical assessment

The car started and drove after the crash. Ben shut it off to cool, then drove it only about 200m, slow, to park it. Short and slow — so we do not believe the engine got hot enough to be damaged.

Do not run it again until the cooling system is fixed. A first look at the open engine bay backs the theory: damage looks limited to the radiator, the fans, and the mounting brackets. We confirm it fully once the new radiator is in.

Parts that look OK

  • Headlights — but the sun-damaged outer covers need replacing.
  • Spot lights.
  • Hood — maybe modest damage, but looks OK.
  • Front side panels — look good; may be slightly bent.

Objectives

  • Repair for minimum cost — but not if it overwhelms Ben.
  • Fit a second-hand alloy bull bar in place of the bumper.
  • Buy parts second-hand from wreckers or Facebook Marketplace.

Success looks like

  • The Pajero starts, runs, and drives.
  • The cooling system holds and does not overheat.
  • Lights and safety gear work.
  • The repairs are sound and it is safe and roadworthy.
  • Ben finishes it feeling good about the work.

Plan

The repair, stage by stage, front to back. One stage at a time. Each has a clear finish line.

Progress so far

  • Broke off the smashed grille to reach the bonnet latch.
  • Unbolted the bent latch. Detached the release cable and sensor wires.
  • The bonnet is open. The engine bay is visible.
  • A first look confirms the theory: damage looks limited to the radiator, fans, and brackets.
  • Next: the car is still parked ~10 min away. Organise a flatbed to move it to Ben's driveway.

Guiding rules

  • One stage at a time. No pressure.
  • Take photos before you touch anything, and as you go.
  • Take video of any non-trivial step or component before you pull it apart — it shows you how it goes back together.
  • Bag every bolt. Put a small note inside each bag: what it is, and a number if you can. Note where it came from.
  • Buy second-hand where safe. Buy new for anything that keeps the car cool or safe.
  • Confirm a part fits before you buy it.
  • Stop and ask if something feels wrong or too hard.

The stages

00Set upNext

A safe, tidy place to work.

  • Flat ground, chock the wheels, handbrake on.
  • Light, a parts bench, boxes, bags, a marker.
  • Get the NT Pajero workshop manual. Start a phone photo album.

Done when: the car is safe and the workspace is ready.

01Open the bonnetDone

Get the bonnet open to see the damage.

  • Broke off the grille to reach the latch.
  • Unbolted the latch. Detached the cable and sensor wires.

Done: bonnet open, engine bay visible.

02Get the car homeNext

Move the car to Ben's driveway. Do not drive it — the radiator is holed and the engine has no cooling. Ten minutes on the road could cook it.

  • Book a flatbed (tilt-tray) tow. Ring 2–3 local operators for a short move. Rough cost A$100–A$180.
  • Say it rolls, steers, and brakes — that makes loading easy and can lower the price.

Done when: the car sits in Ben's driveway, ready to strip.

03Strip the frontIn progress

Remove the smashed parts. Reveal what is behind them.

  • Grille removed. ✓
  • Drain any coolant left — catch it, keep it off the drive and away from pets. It is toxic.
  • Remove the bumper bar, the broken fans, the holed radiator.
  • Photograph and bag every bolt. Number each bag and say the number in the photo or video, so picture, clip, and bag all match.

Done when: the front is stripped to the support panel.

04Assess hidden damageTo do

Know the full picture before spending money.

  • The radiator support bracket is badly bent and needs replacing — check how far the damage spreads.
  • Check the A/C condenser and the intercooler and pipes. They sit in the crush zone.
  • Look for oil in the coolant, or coolant in the oil.
  • Write down every damaged part.

Done when: we have a full damage list and know the engine is sound.

05Source the partsTo do

Gather every part before major refitting.

  • Confirm each part number against the car.
  • Second-hand where safe; new or known-good for anything that keeps the engine cool.
  • Second-hand alloy bull bar; new headlight covers.

Done when: every part is in hand and checked.

06Replace the support structureTo do

A straight, solid frame for the radiator.

  • Replace the bent bracket with a good used one.
  • Check it is square. Treat bare metal against rust.

Done when: the radiator will sit true.

07Fit the cooling systemTo do

A cooling system that holds and works.

  • Fit the new radiator and fans. Reconnect the wiring.
  • Fit condenser and intercooler if replaced. New hoses and clamps.
  • Fill with correct coolant. Bleed the air out.

Done when: the system is fitted, full, and bled.

08Fit the bull barTo do

The alloy bull bar mounted in place of the bumper.

  • Check it fits the NT mounts. Bolt to the chassis rails.
  • Check it sits level and clears the bonnet and lights. Refit spot lights.

Done when: the bull bar is mounted, level, and secure.

09Refit the front trimTo do

The front looks finished and the lights work.

  • Fit the grille and new headlight covers.
  • Refit and aim the headlights. Check every front light.

Done when: the front is trimmed and every light works.

10Test the coolingTo do

Prove the engine stays cool and dry.

  • Start cold. Watch the temperature. Check the fans cut in.
  • Check for leaks. Pressure-test if a tester is on hand.
  • Check again for oil and water mixing.

Done when: it reaches normal temperature, holds it, and does not leak.

11Roadworthy checkTo do

Assess the repairs for roadworthiness. Make sure the work is safe and sound before the car goes back on the road.

  • Check every light works: head, spot, park, indicator, brake. Check the horn and wipers.
  • Check nothing in the front is loose — tug the bull bar, radiator, and fittings.
  • Check for coolant and oil leaks. Check the coolant holds its level after a run.
  • Confirm it steers, brakes, and drives straight.

Done when: the repairs are sound and the car is safe and roadworthy.

12FinishTo do

Close the job.

  • Final photos. Dispose of old coolant and parts safely.
  • Update the documents. Ben drives his Pajero.

Done when: the car is back on the road and Ben is happy.

Open questions

  • Is the A/C condenser damaged? Confirm in Stage 3.
  • Is the intercooler or its piping damaged? Confirm in Stage 3.
  • Does the bull bar need a mount kit specific to the NT Pajero?
  • Do the front side panels need straightening, or do they pass as-is?
