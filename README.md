# Pajero Repair Project

Repair Ben's 2009 Mitsubishi Pajero. It ran into the back of another car and smashed the front end. Ben does the work in his driveway. The job stays cheap, safe, and paced so it does not overwhelm him.

## Goal

Coordinate the repair of Ben's Mitsubishi Pajero. Get it back on the road, legal and roadworthy, for a low cost.

## The car

* 2009 Mitsubishi Pajero VR-X
* Diesel
* NT Series
* Australia

## Constraints

* **Budget:** up to $4,000. Favour cheap second-hand parts.
* **Timeline:** 1 to 3 months.
* **Registration:** registered now, and must stay roadworthy.
* **Airbags:** did not deploy. No airbag work needed.
* **Location:** Ben's driveway, in front of his garage. In Sydney, Australia.

## Who does the work

* Ben does the work.
* He has limited experience repairing cars.
* He is keen. He wants to learn and enjoy this.

## How we work

* Speak in plain, practical terms. Short sentences. The Hemingway way.
* Break the work into small steps.
* One task at a time. No pressure.

## Damage

The Pajero ran into the back of another car. That car had a tow bar. The tow bar pushed into the front of the Pajero. It crushed the front grille, went through the radiator fans, and into the radiator.

* Front grille crushed.
* Radiator fans broken.
* Radiator damaged. A large amount of coolant spilled on the road after the crash. The radiator is holed and will need replacing.
* Bumper bar crushed.
* Support bracket between the radiator and front grille badly bent. The radiator hangs off this bracket. It needs replacing.
* Bonnet latch bent. It would not release the bonnet.

## Mechanical assessment

After the crash the car started and drove. It seemed to run fine. But it had lost its coolant, so it had no engine cooling. Ben shut it off and let it cool. He then drove it only about 200m around the corner to park it long term. That run was short and slow. So we do not believe the engine got hot enough to be damaged.

Because it started and drove without other trouble, we suspect the main mechanical damage is limited to the radiator and its fans. We have since broken off the grille and opened the bonnet. A first look at the engine bay backs this up. The damage looks limited to the radiator, the fans, and the mounting brackets. We confirm it fully once the front is stripped and the new radiator is in.

## Parts that look OK

* Headlights. The outer covers need replacing. They are sun-damaged.
* Spot lights.
* Hood. Maybe modest damage, but looks OK.
* Front side panels. They look good. They may be slightly bent.

## Objectives

* Repair for minimum cost. But not if it becomes too hard for Ben to manage.
* Ben buys a second-hand alloy bull bar to replace the front bumper bar.
* Buy replacement parts second-hand from wreckers or Facebook Marketplace.

## Success looks like

* The Pajero starts, runs, and drives.
* The cooling system holds and does not overheat.
* Lights and safety gear work.
* The repairs are sound and the car is safe and roadworthy.
* Ben finishes it feeling good about the work.
